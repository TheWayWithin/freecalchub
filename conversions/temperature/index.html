Temperature is a measure of the hotness or coldness of an object or substance. Temperature conversions are vital in science, engineering, meteorology, and daily life because different parts of the world and various scientific disciplines use different scales (Celsius, Fahrenheit, Kelvin, Rankine), requiring accurate translation for consistency and understanding.

The most common temperature scales are Celsius (°C), used widely around the world and in science; and Fahrenheit (°F), primarily used in the United States. Kelvin (K) is an absolute temperature scale used in scientific contexts.

The General Temperature Converter is designed to convert between Celsius, Fahrenheit, Kelvin, and Rankine scales. It provides a versatile tool for various temperature conversion needs.

To convert Celsius to Fahrenheit, you multiply the Celsius temperature by 9/5 and then add 32. Our Celsius to Fahrenheit Converter will perform this calculation quickly for you.

The Kelvin scale is an absolute thermodynamic temperature scale where absolute zero (0 K) is the theoretical point at which all molecular motion stops. It is primarily used in scientific and engineering applications, particularly in fields like physics, chemistry, and cryogenics, as it avoids negative numbers and simplifies calculations involving gas laws.

The Rankine scale is an absolute temperature scale similar to Kelvin, but it uses the Fahrenheit degree increment. Absolute zero on the Rankine scale is 0 °R, which is equivalent to -459.67 °F. It is primarily used in engineering systems, particularly in the United States, where calculations involving absolute temperatures are necessary in conjunction with Fahrenheit-based measurements.
