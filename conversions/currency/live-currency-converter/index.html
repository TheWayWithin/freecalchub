Understanding Exchange Rates

An exchange rate is the value of one country's currency expressed in terms of another country's currency. These rates fluctuate constantly based on a multitude of factors in the global foreign exchange (forex) market, including supply and demand, economic performance, interest rates set by central banks, geopolitical events, and market speculation.

This calculator typically uses mid-market rates. The mid-market rate is the midpoint between the buy (bid) and sell (ask) prices of two currencies in the wholesale market. It's considered the 'fairest' rate but is generally not available directly to consumers. When you exchange currency at a bank, an exchange bureau, or through a money transfer service, they will typically add a margin or 'spread' to this rate, and may also charge commission fees. This is how they make a profit and cover their operational costs.

Tips for Getting the Best Exchange Rates

  • Compare Providers: Don't settle for the first option. Banks, credit unions, online money transfer services, and currency exchange bureaus all offer different rates and fees.
  • Beware of "No Fee" Claims: Services claiming "no fees" often build their profit into a less favorable exchange rate. Always compare the final amount you'll receive.
  • Avoid Airport Exchanges: Airport kiosks are convenient but typically offer the worst exchange rates and highest fees.
  • ATMs Abroad: Withdrawing cash from a local ATM in your destination country using your debit card can often provide good rates, but be aware of your bank's foreign transaction fees and local ATM fees.
