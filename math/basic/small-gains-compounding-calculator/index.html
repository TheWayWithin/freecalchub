How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter Your Starting Balance: Input the initial amount you're starting with. This is your principal investment.
  2. Select Compounding Frequency: Choose how often your returns compound. Daily is common for trading strategies, while monthly suits typical investments.
  3. Set Your Average Return: Enter your expected average return per period as a percentage. For example, 0.35 means 0.35% per day/week/month.
  4. Choose Time Horizon: Enter the number of periods (days, weeks, or months) you want to calculate.
  5. Add Optional Deposits: If you plan to add money regularly, enter the deposit amount and choose whether deposits occur at the beginning or end of each period.
  6. Click Calculate: View your final balance, total profit, effective annual return, and a period-by-period breakdown with a growth chart.

Understanding Small Gains Compounding

Compounding is often called the "eighth wonder of the world" because it turns small, consistent gains into significant wealth over time. The key insight is that each period's return is applied to your growing balance, not just your original investment.

The Math Behind It

For pure compounding without deposits, the formula is:

A = P × (1 + r)n

Where A is the final amount, P is the principal (starting balance), r is the rate per period as a decimal, and n is the number of periods.

With Regular Deposits (Ordinary Annuity)

When you add regular deposits at the end of each period:

A = P(1+r)n + C × [(1+r)n - 1] / r

Where C is the deposit amount per period.

Effective Annual Return (EAR)

The EAR converts your per-period return to an annual equivalent, making it easy to compare strategies:

  • Daily compounding: EAR = (1 + r)365 - 1
  • Weekly compounding: EAR = (1 + r)52 - 1
  • Monthly compounding: EAR = (1 + r)12 - 1

Real-World Applications

Trading Strategy Analysis

Traders often aim for small daily gains (e.g., 0.3-0.5% per day). This calculator shows how those gains compound over weeks and months, helping you set realistic expectations and goals.

Investment Planning

Model different scenarios: What if you invest $5,000 with 1% monthly returns? How much do regular $200 monthly deposits add to your final balance?

Goal Setting

Work backwards from a goal. If you want to reach $50,000 in 2 years, what daily or weekly return rate do you need to achieve starting from $10,000?

Simple growth means you earn interest on your original starting amount only. Compound growth means you earn interest on your starting amount plus any interest already earned (and deposits if you add them). This calculator uses compounding, which is why small gains can lead to surprisingly large differences over longer periods.

If you enter a regular deposit amount, the calculator assumes you add that amount once per period (for example, every day, every week, or every month depending on your selection). Each deposit then grows at the same rate for however many periods remain. The calculator grows your original balance over the full period, grows each deposit for its remaining time, and adds everything together to show your final balance.
